# Reproducing the component figures in Parisi et al. (2025)

Run from the thermopt root:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe development/turbomachinery/paper_figures.py
```

This creates **Figures 1-7** as PNG and SVG, an [overview](../results/paper_figures/overview.png), calculated-curve CSV, and [numerical comparison report](../results/paper_figures/comparison.md) under `development/turbomachinery/results/paper_figures/`. Use `--points 401` for smoother model curves or `--output PATH` to choose a destination. Generated files are ignored by Git; the scripts and independently extracted references are retained.

Calculated curves use the same small correlation functions called by the production compressor and turbine models. The compressor velocity-ratio correlation includes the correction to printed Equation 23 described below. References are published PDF graphics, not data generated by thermopt. Mean-line points and literature correlations are copied from those graphics for context; their underlying solvers are not reimplemented.

| Figure | Quantity reproduced | Production equations | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unshrouded, vaned compressor baseline polytropic efficiency | 12-14 and Table 4 | Matches the published Aungier curve. The Casey curve and already-shifted mean-line markers are PDF references. |
| 2 | Compressor work coefficient at reference 40-degree backsweep | 12 and 17 | Matches the reference curve; the published mean-line points are already corrected to the reference backsweep. |
| 3 | Compressor Mach-loss effect for pressure ratios 1.1, 3, 5 and 10 | 9, 11, 12, 14, 18-20 | Close reconstruction with the documented perfect-gas/Mach-only assumptions below. |
| 4 | Relative inlet velocity divided by peripheral speed | Corrected 23 / Figure 4 | Matches the published curve and mean-line trend; the erroneous printed coefficients are documented below and in the production docstring. |
| 5 | Diameter divided by blade chord, corrected to reference backsweep | 24-25 | Matches the published reference line. |
| 6 | Baseline rotor total-to-total turbine efficiency | 29 | Matches Figure 6 with cubic coefficient **0.5**, retaining the comment about Table 6's typo. |
| 7 | Baseline rotor total-to-static turbine efficiency | 29, 36-37 | Matches the plotted curve, using the rotor-exit kinetic-energy fraction. |

The maximum absolute errors in Figures 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 and 7 are below $6.4\times10^{-5}$ in their dimensionless plotted quantities. These are comparisons with the authors' drawn correlation curves, not residuals against experimental observations. The source mean-line designs intentionally scatter around the correlations. Matching a curve is not independent thermodynamic or aerodynamic validation.

## Figure 4: correction to printed Equation 23

Fitting the 25 vertices of the published Figure 4 correlation gives

$$
\frac{w}{U} \approx 0.38 + 3\phi - 7.8\phi^2.
$$

The actual fitted quadratic coefficients are approximately $[-7.7999449,\ 2.9999665,\ 0.3800016]$. Printed Equation 23 contains an error and instead gives

$$
\frac{w}{U} = 0.42 + 5\phi - 14\phi^2.
$$

The original thermopt implementation followed that printed expression. The production model now uses **$w/U=0.38+3\phi-7.8\phi^2$**, consistent with Figure 4 and its supporting mean-line markers, and its docstring explains the paper error. At $\phi=0.15$, the corrected value is approximately 0.6545; the old expression gave 0.8550. This submodel feeds both the actual and reference chord Reynolds numbers used in the compressor efficiency correction.

The corrected expression has an RMSE of approximately 0.00509 against the 72 visible mean-line markers across the three backsweep groups, compared with 0.15968 for the printed expression. The model correction follows that published evidence. It does not turn digitized graphics into independent raw-data validation. The original publication-vector coordinates in `curves.json` remain unchanged.

There is also a smaller internal inconsistency in Figure 1: its legend states a 1.4 percentage-point shift for the 20-degree mean-line designs, whereas Equation 16 gives 1.2 points. We retain the marker positions as published and do not correct them a second time.

## Figure 3 assumptions

The paper introduces Figure 3 to illustrate the Mach correction before introducing the Reynolds correction. The caption does not specify a complete real-fluid design point. This reconstruction therefore isolates the baseline and Mach terms, holding other corrections at their reference values. It uses `jaxprop.FluidPerfectGas("Air", T_ref=298)` to obtain $\gamma=c_p/c_v$ at 298 K and 1 bar, consistent with the reference conditions in Table 3.

For a perfect gas, Equations 9 and 11 give

$$
\frac{T_{out}}{T_{in}} = PR^{(\gamma-1)/(\gamma\eta_p)},
\qquad
Ma_u^2 = \frac{T_{out}/T_{in}-1}{(\gamma-1)I}.
$$

The script solves $\eta_p=\eta_{base}+\delta\eta_{Ma}$ for the highest-efficiency root. This reproduces all four published trends. The maximum interior-vertex difference is approximately **0.022 efficiency percentage points**, at pressure ratio 10. The report labels Figure 3 as an approximate reconstruction under stated assumptions, using a separate 0.05-point criterion. Other figures use $2\times10^{-4}$ in their plotted quantity as an extraction/reproduction criterion. These criteria do not replace the tighter machine/cycle regression tolerances. Affected compressor and cycle reference values are deliberately refreshed for the authorized Equation 23 correction.

This calculation is not a full HEOS compressor design sweep. Absolute size, inlet pressure and temperature cancel from the perfect-gas Mach-only equations. The earlier [compressor demonstration](../demo_compressor.py) remains the full-state design-point example with all corrections. Where no efficiency root is found in the searched interval, the plot has a gap and the CSV explicitly records it; values are not clipped to a desired trend.

## Turbine interpretation

Figures 6 and 7 plot **rotor baseline** efficiencies. Applying the default scalloping penalty, diffuser loss and Reynolds correction would produce different curves. Figure 7 uses

$$
\eta_{ts,rot}=\frac{\eta_{tt,rot}}{1+\kappa_{rot}},
$$

rather than the default overall-machine diffuser factor $1+0.35\kappa_{rot}$. The full turbine example is [demo_turbine.py](../demo_turbine.py). Dashed calculated sections outside specific speed 0.35-0.85 are explicit extrapolations shown only to match the published plotting range.

## Reference data and attribution

Source: Simone Parisi, Roberto Agromayor, Angelo La Seta, Kenny Krogh Nielsen and Fredrik Haglind, *Advancing non-dimensional models for radial turbomachinery applied to pumped thermal energy storage*, Energy Conversion and Management 342 (2025), 120085. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enconman.2025.120085); [open-access DTU PDF](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/406859073/1-s2.0-S0196890425006090-main.pdf). Published graphics are reused under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Changes made here: extraction of vector coordinates, axis calibration, relabeling and overlay with calculated curves. The original paper and its authors do not endorse these changes.

[curves.json](curves.json) records the DOI, download URL, PDF SHA-256, page numbers, axis bounds, source path indices, extracted polylines and visible marker centers. It includes the Casey, Aungier and Kofskey reference curves exactly as drawn in this paper; it does not claim to reproduce their original datasets or implement those authors' models. Mean-line points are digitized publication markers, not experimental measurements.

PDF pages 7-10 correspond to printed article pages 6-9 because the DTU copy includes a cover page. The SVG path coordinates and axis ticks were checked visually. Circular/diamond markers use their symmetric bounds, triangles use their vertex centroid, and legend markers are excluded. Figure 4 has only 24 visible markers per backsweep because the lowest-flow point is outside the plotted range. Clipped line endpoints are excluded from curve-error statistics because their positions can be interpolated by the PDF renderer.

To repeat the extraction, install Poppler's `pdftocairo` separately and provide the reviewed PDF:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe development/turbomachinery/extract_paper_data.py PATH_TO_DTU_PDF --overwrite
```

The extractor verifies the source hash and refuses other PDF layouts. A changed source file requires reviewing page indices, paths and axis calibration. **No PDF library or new runtime dependency was added to thermopt.** Plot generation and tests read the checked-in JSON and need neither Poppler nor network access. Tests never regenerate it.
